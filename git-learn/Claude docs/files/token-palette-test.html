What I see, now that they're side by side

A · Violet is the most immediately striking and the first one your eye goes to — which is exactly the problem at ten opens a day. It also has the structural flaw: violet is doing brand duty and state duty at once, so "live" stops being a signal. And it carries the DeFi accent, which is the wrong association for a product whose promise is that nothing surprising will happen.

B · Deep green fixes the association instantly — this is the register WhatsApp, Signal and every bank already occupy, and green means permission almost everywhere in the world. It feels calmer than A within about two seconds of looking. But it inherits the same double-duty flaw: if green is the brand, green can't also be the thing that tells you a door is open.

C · Neutral + state is the one I'd ship. Black is the brand, so the interface itself has no opinion; green appears only where a door is live, amber only where one is closing, red only on revoke. Every colour on screen carries information and nothing is decorative. It's the quietest of the three and the only one where the palette gets more useful as you add more tokens — with twenty doors open, A and B become noise while C stays a readable status board.

Notice the chat strips too. In C your own messages are near-black, which is the highest-legibility pairing there is, and the read receipt is the single green mark on the screen. That's a surface you can look at for two years.

Test 01
The squint test
Half-close your eyes. In C, the only thing that survives is the live door — which is the one thing that should. In A and B, the whole screen survives equally.
Test 02
The twenty-token test
Imagine each screen with twenty tokens instead of three. C stays a status board. A and B turn into a wall of brand colour where nothing stands out.
Test 03
The screenshot test
Picture each in the App Store next to WhatsApp and Signal. C reads as a serious tool. B reads as a messenger. A reads as a crypto app.

Where I could be wrong: C is more restrained than most consumer apps in India, and restraint can read as unfinished to a mainstream audience — CRED and Jupiter both won on richness, not quiet. If you want warmth without losing the discipline, the honest middle is C with a slightly deeper, warmer green and marginally more of it. But I'd keep the rule intact: the brand is black, colour means state.
