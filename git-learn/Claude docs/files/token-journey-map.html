Token · journey map
Customer journey · v7

Two people. One wall. Only the token crosses.

Ashish installs the app and lives inside the vault. Rahul — the buyer, the delivery agent, the contractor — never installs anything and only ever sees a web page. Nearly every design decision in Token comes from keeping those two paths apart, and being deliberate about the handful of moments where something is allowed to pass between them.

01

The journey

Read top to bottom. Left of the seal is the owner's app; right of it is the holder's browser. The badges spanning the line are the only points where anything actually crosses.

S0 · TRIGGER

The annoyance that starts everything

before install

He isn't shopping for a privacy app. He's irritated by something concrete:

  • Listed the Swift on OLX — still fielding calls four months later
  • One delivery agent now WhatsApps him personally
  • One broker passed his number to six other brokers
  • A test drive turned into permanent loan spam
"This is just what having a phone number costs."
What has to land: you can hand out contact without handing out your number — and take it back afterwards.
Category doesn't exist yet
S1 · ONBOARD

Three slides, one metaphor

s-onboard
  • Sealed by default — no number to leak, no inbox to spam
  • Mint a token to open a door — permission, not identity
  • Revoke it and it's gone — the path to you stops existing
"So… it's a second-number app?"
Job of this stage: kill that comparison on sight. Doosra gives you another number. Token gives you revocable permissions — the hotel key card is the whole pitch in one image.
Low drop-off
S2 · VAULT

Handing a privacy app your real number

s-phone → s-otp

One field, one OTP. No password, no email, no name, no profile photo.

"Wait — I'm giving my number to the app that promises to hide it?"
On-screen answer: the number secures the account and routes bridged calls back to him; it is never shown to anyone. Backed by the trust strip — no contact sync ever, encrypted at rest, AWS Mumbai, DPDP compliant.
Biggest ask before any value
S3 · THE EMPTY ROOM

The promise delivered looks like an app doing nothing

s-chats · empty state

He lands on Chats. It's empty. The copy reads Perfectly silent. A toast confirms: sealed, nobody can reach you now.

"Good. …Now what?"
Design response: exactly one instruction and one button, plus a demo-world escape hatch for the curious. Anything more competes with the only action that matters.
Highest drop-off in the funnel
S4 · FIRST MINT

Three taps, and the idea becomes an object

sheet-mint

FAB → template (Marketplace sale pre-selected) → Mint & share. Rules arrive pre-set: chat, call and SMS, seven days, 9am–7pm. Naming it is optional; the rules drawer stays shut unless he wants it.

If the template includes voice and he isn't verified yet, the identity check appears inside the sheet and completes there — no ejection from the flow.

"That was it?"
The delight moment: the pass card — dark, holographic edge, monospace code. It has to feel like a physical artifact he now owns.
Every deferrable decision deferred
S5 · SHARE

Sending a stranger a link he's never seen before

mint result · copy / WhatsApp / QR / NFC

He pastes it into the OLX chat with Rahul.

"Will he think this is weird? Will it even work?"
Mitigation: Preview what they see — he can inspect the holder's page before sending. One button that removes most of the social risk. The remaining friction isn't in the UI; it's the sentence he has to write alongside the link.
Risk lands on the other side
the link crosses the seal →
H1 · ARRIVAL

The single most fragile moment in the product

tokn.app/t/TOKN-8GH2-LP4X

A link from a stranger, in an OLX thread, that isn't a phone number.

"What is this? Is this a scam? Why won't he just give me his number?"
Constraint: under two seconds on 4G, no install, no signup, no cookie wall. Every extra step here costs a redemption — and an unredeemed token means the whole owner journey was wasted.
Reads as phishing by default
H2 · COMPREHENSION

Four seconds to turn suspicion into understanding

grant card · channel list

An avatar, "A door to Ashish", the token code, and a green strip: valid, expires in 22 days, reachable 9am–7pm. Then the channels — secure chat, voice call, SMS bridge — each in plain language.

Anything the token doesn't allow stays visible but locked: not allowed by this token.

"Oh — I can see exactly what I'm allowed to do."
Why show the locked rows: they give the permission a visible shape. Hiding them would make the page feel arbitrary; showing them makes it feel governed.
Legibility decides everything
H3 · USE

It just works — and his number stays hidden too

voice bridge · secure chat · biz verification

He taps voice and it connects through a bridge, or types in the chat and Ashish's phone lights up. For business-only tokens, the company verifies an identity once before anything unlocks; individuals can't redeem those at all.

"Wait, he can't see my number either?"
Reciprocity is the conversion lever. Saying this out loud on the page turns the holder from a reluctant participant into someone who benefits — worth more than any trust badge.
Value proves itself
← contact passes, identity does not
S6 · FIRST CONTACT

The real aha — value passing through an intact wall

s-chats → s-chat · incoming call

A banner slides in, the tab badge lights, the thread row carries a live token chip. Inside the thread, a strip under the header holds the code, the expiry, and pause / revoke. If Rahul calls instead, it rings as a bridged call — both numbers hidden, both directions.

"He's talking to me and he has no idea who I am."
Not the mint — this. Minting is a promise; this is the proof. A minted token that never reaches this stage means the share step failed, not the product.
Payoff moment
S7 · LIVING WITH IT

Weeks two and three, where products quietly die

s-doors · s-calls
  • The delivery token expires by itself after 24 hours — no action needed
  • An uncertain one gets paused: reversible, no guilt, no conversation
  • Calls logs bridged, missed, and greyed bounced rows — no token, never rang
"Three people tried to reach me this week and none of them got through."
Honest caveat: bounces are attempts on dead links and retired relay numbers — not magical detection of every stranger. The copy must not overclaim, or the first sceptical user catches it.
Silence erodes the habit
S8 · REVOKE

The climax: silence, restored

sheet-revoke

The car is sold. Rahul is now a stranger with a phone. Revoke from the chat strip — a confirmation sheet shows the pass card, warns that it's permanent, and offers pause instead. He confirms.

The thread seals. The card goes grey with the code struck through.

No block, no argument, no awkward conversation. Just quiet.
Rule this enforces everywhere: irreversible actions never happen on a single unconfirmed tap.
Everything before this was setup
the door stops existing →
H4 · THE CLOSED DOOR

"This token no longer exists"

dead state · paused state

He opens the link again and finds it dead — or paused, if Ashish only wanted a break. There is no number to try, no inbox to find, and deliberately no request access button to press.

Rejected, but with nothing to argue with and nobody to badger.
Design principle: silence must be a complete answer. Any appeal mechanism re-imports exactly the social pressure the product exists to remove.
Clean ending by design
S9 · EXPANSION

Circles — the hardest ask in the app

s-circles

Family. D-204 Society. One shared token, same code grammar, marked shared — everyone in one private space, and nobody exchanges numbers, not even with each other.

"Could this replace the society WhatsApp group?"
Honest read: it competes with WhatsApp groups on WhatsApp's home turf and needs other people to install. The society case — where members genuinely don't want each other's numbers — is the strongest wedge. High adoption risk, near-zero harm if unused.
Depends on other people
S10 · THE WALL

Paying at the moment of wanting more

s-plan → checkout

Free covers five live tokens, chat, and two circles. The paywall arrives on the sixth token, or the first voice bridge, email relay, or location drop.

"₹99 to never deal with this again? Fine."
Non-negotiable: sealing, minting, chatting and revoking stay free forever. That's the promise. Charge for volume and channels, never for the core loop.
Value already felt
S11 · EXIT

A frictionless door out is a trust signal

s-you → export · delete

Export the archive, or delete everything: every token dies instantly, conversations and circles are erased, the vault is wiped. Nobody — including the company — can reach him or rebuild it.

Why it's a feature: for a privacy product the delete button is part of the pitch, not a leak. DPDP makes it mandatory regardless.
Ends the way it promised
02

Three moments that decide whether Token lives

RISK 01

The empty room

S3 · minutes after signup

Perfect protection is visually identical to a broken app. He seals himself, sees nothing, and closes it. There may be no second session.

Currently held by: one instruction, one button, and a demo world. Measure it as first token minted within 24 hours — this is the activation number.
RISK 02

The stranger's first click

H1 · outside the app entirely

A link from someone he met on OLX, that isn't a phone number. Four seconds of suspicion carry more product risk than every screen in the app combined.

Currently held by: preview-before-send, a page that loads instantly, and visibly locked channels. Measure it as redemptions per minted token.
RISK 03

Week three

S7 · after the first token dies

Nothing happens. No one knocks. The app has honoured its promise so completely that it slides off the home screen.

Currently held by: the bounce log and expiry nudges — thin. This is the retention problem still open in the design.

04

Open questions this map surfaces

Q1 · SHARE
Should the app write the sentence that goes with the link?

A human line pasted alongside — "use this to reach me, it's my contact link" — may do more for redemption than any UI change. Auto-generate it with every share, or leave it to him?

Q2 · RETENTION
What happens on a week with zero activity?

A weekly "you stayed sealed, three attempts bounced" digest is the obvious loop — and the fastest route back to the vanity stat the teardown already killed. Needs a sharper idea.

Q3 · SCOPE
Is Circles a v1 feature or a v2 wedge?

Retained by decision. The society case is real, but it's the only part of the product that can't succeed without other people installing.

Q4 · ACTIVATION
Should first run include a guided mint?

A simulated buyer who redeems and messages back would collapse S3 → S6 from days into ninety seconds — at the cost of putting something fake inside a product built entirely on trust.
