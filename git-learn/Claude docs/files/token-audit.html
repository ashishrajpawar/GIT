Token · v7 audit
Audit · v7 prototype · 25 July 2026

The product thinking is sound. The shipping layer doesn't exist yet.

I ran this against the actual source of token-v7.html rather than from memory. The core loop — mint, share, redeem, revoke — is coherent and genuinely differentiated. What's missing is almost entirely the layer between "a working demo" and "an app a stranger can install": failure states, accessibility, moderation, recovery, and the store-compliance surface.

Two findings are severe enough to change the product model, not just the build: token links are freely forwardable, and push notifications leak the very thing the app exists to hide. Both are below in P0.

00

What's genuinely strong

Don't rebuild these — they're the product

  • The token strip in the thread. No other messenger shows the permission that allows a conversation, inside that conversation. This is the single most original thing in the app.
  • Revoke → confirmation → "Pause instead". Correct handling of an irreversible action, with an escape hatch aimed at the real emotion behind most revokes.
  • The dead holder page. "This token no longer exists" is the most satisfying moment in the product, and the decision to make unknown codes render identically is exactly right.
  • Two-tap mint with templates. Seven decisions reduced to a default that works. The inline KYC callout — doorway rather than dead end — is a genuine improvement over v4.
  • Showing locked channels on the holder page. Displaying what the token doesn't grant gives the permission a visible shape. Counter-intuitive and correct.
  • Vocabulary discipline. Down from 13 invented terms to about five. This is why v7 reads as a tool rather than a lifestyle app.
01

P0 — blocks launch

Store rejection, legal exposure, or a promise the product can't keep.
P0-01

A token link is freely forwardable — the permission model has no holder

product modelcore promise

The entire product is built on "I minted this for Rahul." But what Rahul receives is a URL. He can paste it into a broker group of forty people and every one of them walks through the same door. The app even states this plainly in the D1 rules grid — Audience: anyone with the link — but nothing anywhere handles the consequence.

The marketplace case is exactly where this bites: the buyer who never bought forwards your link to three dealer friends, and you now have four strangers in a thread you believe belongs to one person.

  • No indication in the timeline that a second device redeemed
  • No alert, no cap, no binding
  • The owner's mental model ("Rahul's token") and the system's behaviour ("anyone's token") have quietly diverged
Fix

Bind on first redemption. Issue a holder credential (httpOnly cookie + device fingerprint) the first time the link is opened; subsequent devices get a distinct state. Then give the owner three things: a timeline entry "Redeemed by a second device", a notification, and a mint-time rule "Lock to the first person who opens it" — on by default for the Marketplace and Contractor templates, off for Delivery, where a dispatcher legitimately hands the link to whichever driver takes the run.

P0-02

Push notifications leak exactly what the app exists to hide

privacynotifications

The spec's notification copy is Rahul messaged via TOKN-8GH2-LP4X. On a lock screen, in a meeting, on a shared desk, that displays a name, a message preview, and a token code to anyone glancing at the phone. A privacy product that broadcasts its contents to the room has an contradiction at its centre.

It's worse on Android Wear / CarPlay mirroring, and worse again if the phone is the thing being handed around — which is precisely the scenario A6 App Lock exists for.

Fix

Content-hidden by default, not opt-in. The default push reads "Someone used one of your tokens" with no name, no preview, no code. A settings toggle — "Show details in notifications" — lets the user opt into richer previews knowingly. Never include the token code in a push payload at all; it's the credential.

P0-03

Link previews burn the token before the holder ever opens it

privacyintegration

The primary share path is WhatsApp. The moment the link is pasted, Meta's servers fetch that URL to build a preview card. Three consequences, none handled:

  • The token code reaches a third party before the intended holder sees it
  • The preview card renders "A door to Ashish" inside the chat — visible to every participant in a group thread
  • If redemption is ever bound or counted, the crawler consumes the first use
Fix

Serve no Open Graph tags on /t/{code}, or a deliberately generic one ("Token — secure contact link") with no avatar and no name. Detect crawler user-agents and serve a static shell that never touches redemption state. Same rule for the circle invite links.

P0-04

No account recovery — and no decision about whether there should be

product modelsupport

The phone number is the sole identity. Lose the SIM, change carriers badly, or have the number recycled, and every token, thread and circle is unreachable forever. Grep confirms it: zero occurrences of recovery, backup, or even logout anywhere in v7 — you can delete the account, but you cannot sign out of it.

This is a genuine fork, not an oversight to patch. A support-operated recovery path means Anthropic-style trust ("we can restore you") but also means a human can reassign your vault, which is a backdoor into the exact thing you sell.

Fix

Decide explicitly and say it out loud in onboarding. Recommended: generate 8 recovery codes at signup, force the user to save one, and make recovery cryptographic rather than human. Support can never restore an account — and that becomes a marketing line, not an apology. Separately: ship a logout. Its absence will read as a bug in the first review.

P0-05

User-generated content with no moderation pipeline — automatic store rejection

app storelegal

Token has open chat between strangers. Both stores treat that as UGC and both require the same four things before approval. v7 has a Report button that calls askRevoke() — it kills the token locally and nothing is ever sent anywhere.

  • A method to filter objectionable content — server-side, before delivery
  • A mechanism to report that reaches a human, with a stated response window (Apple expects 24 hours)
  • A mechanism to block abusive users — distinct from revoking a token, because the abuser can be handed a second link
  • A published contact for users to reach the developer

There is also no EULA with a zero-tolerance clause, which Apple asks for explicitly on UGC apps.

Fix

Build a real report pipeline: report → server record with the message context → triage queue → action. Add a persistent blocklist keyed to the holder credential, not the token, so a blocked person stays out across future links. Publish a support email and a 24-hour SLA in the store listing.

P0-06

No privacy policy, terms, or in-app legal surface

app storeDPDP

Grep: zero occurrences of "privacy policy" in v7, and no help or support route. Both stores hard-require a reachable privacy policy URL; Play additionally requires the Data Safety form and App Store the privacy nutrition labels, both of which must match actual behaviour.

For India specifically, DPDP requires a published grievance officer with contact details, and a consent notice at collection describing purpose. Neither exists.

Fix

Add E4 Settings rows: Privacy policy · Terms · Grievance officer · Contact support. Draft the Data Safety declarations from the real schema — and note that Token's answers are unusually favourable (no contact sync, no ad IDs, no third-party sharing beyond processors). That form is a marketing asset for this product, not just paperwork.

P0-07

Zero failure states — the app assumes the network always works

reliabilitycraft
grep token-v7.html → loading: 0 · skeleton: 0 · offline: 0 · navigator.onLine: 0 · error: 0 · retry: 0 · try/catch: 1 (clipboard only)

Every action in v7 succeeds instantly and silently. A real app on an Indian 4G connection in a basement does not. The dangerous case is specific: a revoke that appears to succeed but never reached the server. The user believes the door is dead. It isn't.

Fix

Three rules. One: optimistic UI for messages only — never for token state changes. Two: pause, revoke and mint show a real pending state and a real failure with retry. Three: when offline, disable token mutations outright with the banner "You're offline — token changes need a connection." Skeletons on every list, not spinners.

P0-08

Accessibility is below the floor both stores and Indian enterprise buyers expect

a11yWCAG
aria-label: 22 (icon buttons only) · role: 0 · aria-live: 0 · aria-pressed/expanded: 0
touch targets below 44pt: 15 (36–38px icon buttons) · font sizes below 12px: 53 declarations, smallest 8.5px
--faint #97A3B7 on white ≈ 2.55:1 — fails WCAG AA (4.5:1) and even large-text AA (3:1)

Three distinct problems. Toasts and banners have no aria-live, so a screen-reader user gets no confirmation that a revoke happened. Icon buttons at 36px miss the 44pt/48dp minimum, which matters most for the Pause and Revoke pills in the token strip — small targets adjacent to an irreversible action. And every size is hard-coded in px, so Dynamic Type and Android font scaling do nothing.

Fix

Darken --faint to roughly #6B7A93 (≈4.6:1) and keep 11px as the absolute floor. Scale all type with the OS setting and test at 200%. Give every touch target a 44pt hit area even where the visual is smaller. Add aria-live="polite" to the toast and assertive to the banner. Label every state change, not just every button.

P0-09

Redemption endpoint invites enumeration

securitybackend

Codes are TOKN-XXXX-XXXX. The keyspace is adequate if the characters are truly random, but v7 generates them from Math.random() and the demo codes are visibly patterned. Nothing rate-limits the redemption route, and nothing prevents someone walking the space looking for live doors.

The "unknown code renders identically to revoked" rule is correct and already specced — but it only holds if response timing is also identical, otherwise the difference is measurable.

Fix

Server-side CSPRNG, minimum 8 characters from an unambiguous alphabet (drop O/0, I/1/l). Rate-limit by IP and by prefix, with exponential backoff. Constant-time response for unknown vs dead codes. Log enumeration attempts — and, pleasingly, surface them in the bounce log, where they are genuinely accurate.

P0-10

Voice can't ship on the v1 timeline — and nothing acknowledges that

scopecompliance

Call is presented throughout as a first-class channel, default-on in the Marketplace and Contractor templates. Shipping it requires, in order: a KYC vendor contract, TRAI DLT registration, an Exotel account with number provisioning, CallKit and ConnectionService integration, and the full E5 rejection-path UI that doesn't exist yet. That's months, most of it paperwork you cannot compress.

SMS is no better — DLT template registration applies there too.

Fix

See the shipping recommendation at the end of this audit. In short: make v1 chat-only, keep the Call chip visible but permanently in its "needs verification" state with honest copy, and ship voice as the v1.1 headline. The product remains coherent without it.

P0-11

Deep links aren't wired — the share loop breaks for anyone who has the app

integrationgrowth

Every token link points at tokn.app/t/{code}. That's correct for a holder with no app — the entire premise. But circle invites go to existing users too, and nothing associates the domain with the app: no Universal Links entitlement, no Android App Links intent filter, no assetlinks.json.

Consequence: a Token user tapping a circle invite lands in a browser instead of the app, and the "no install required" promise is never contrasted with the smoother in-app path.

Fix

Ship both association files and claim /t/* and /c/*. The web page stays the canonical experience for strangers; installed users get routed into the app. Handle cold start, warm start, and the case where the app is installed but the user isn't the owner.

02

P1 — breaks trust

Ships without rejection, but erodes the reason the product exists.
P1-01

"Chats" and "Tokens" are two views of one thing

IAnavigation

Every thread has a token; every active token tends to have a thread. Two of the four tabs are therefore the same objects sorted differently, which is why the token chip had to be invented for the thread row — it's a bridge between two views that arguably shouldn't be separate.

It isn't wrong, but it's unvalidated. The specific risk: a user who wants to revoke goes to Tokens, doesn't recognise "Rahul — car sale" as the conversation he just had, and comes back confused.

Fix

Don't restructure on instinct — test it. Give five people the task "stop the person you sold the car to from contacting you" and watch which tab they open. If most go to Chats, the Tokens tab becomes a filter inside Chats rather than a peer, and you drop to three tabs.

P1-02

Token codes are hostile to say out loud

copyreal world

TOKN-8GH2-LP4X is fourteen characters with case ambiguity and character collisions (8/B, 4/A, LP/IP). The moment anyone reads one over a phone call or writes it on a delivery slip, it fails. And the codes appear in copy the holder sees, so they aren't purely internal.

Fix

Keep the code as the machine identifier and stop showing it to holders. The holder page should lead with the label and the URL, not the code. Where a code must be spoken, offer a separate short human form — three dictionary words, or six digits — resolved server-side. Drop O, 0, I, 1 and l from the alphabet regardless.

P1-03

Blocking is attached to tokens, not people

safetymodel

Report kills the token. But the person behind it is unaffected: if they hold a second link — a circle invite, a token you minted for something else, a forwarded link per P0-01 — they're still inside. For a harassment case, "I revoked the token" is not "I am rid of this person."

Fix

Introduce a holder identity — an opaque server-side credential established at first redemption. Blocking targets that, not the token. It also unlocks the thing owners will actually want: "This person has redeemed 3 of your tokens."

P1-04

No message delivery states — trust in a messenger is built on ticks

messaging

v7 renders double ticks on every outbound message unconditionally. There's no sending, sent, delivered, or failed. For a product whose entire proposition is controlling a channel, silently losing a message is disproportionately damaging.

Fix

Real states with a persisted outbox and retry. Note the deliberate asymmetry already in the spec — the holder gets no read receipts from the owner, so ignoring someone stays free. That asymmetry is correct; it just needs to be a decision the code expresses rather than an accident of the prototype.

P1-05

Expiry and reachable hours have no timezone semantics

correctness

"Reachable 9:00–19:00" — whose clock? The owner's, presumably. But the holder page displays that string raw, so a holder in Dubai reads it as local and is refused at what he believes is 10am. "Expires in 22 days" is likewise relative, with no absolute timestamp anywhere.

Fix

Store everything in UTC with the owner's IANA zone. Render for the holder in their local time with the owner's zone noted: "Reachable 09:00–19:00 IST (11:30–21:30 your time)." Show an absolute expiry date under every relative one.

P1-06

Nothing survives a restart — and no persistence decision has been made

architectureprivacy

Expected in a prototype, but the underlying question is unanswered and it's a privacy question, not a technical one: what is stored on the device, and is it encrypted at rest? A privacy app that leaves plaintext message history in an unencrypted SQLite file has a weak spot that a competitor's teardown will find.

Fix

Encrypted local store (SQLCipher or the platform equivalent), key in Keychain/Keystore and gated by App Lock when enabled. Decide the local retention window and state it in the privacy policy — a short one is a feature here.

P1-07

The bounce log's honesty problem is still live

copyintegrity

Flagged before and worth repeating, because it's the finding most likely to be quietly ignored. "Contact attempt without a token — bounced" implies the app detected someone trying to reach you. It didn't. It detected a hit on a dead link or a retired relay number. A stranger with no link generates no event at all.

The first sceptical user works this out, and when they do, they re-evaluate every other claim the app makes.

Fix

Rewrite to what actually happened: "Someone tried a link that no longer works." Less dramatic, entirely true, and it still delivers the feeling of a door holding. With P0-09 in place, enumeration attempts become a second genuinely accurate bounce category.

P1-08

No session management, no logout, no multi-device story

securityexpected
grep → logout: 0 · sign out: 0 · token refresh: 0

There is no way to end a session, see active devices, or revoke a session remotely. For an app whose vocabulary is revocation, being unable to revoke your own sessions is an obvious inconsistency — and it's the first thing a security-minded reviewer will check.

Fix

Short-lived access token with refresh rotation, refresh token in secure storage. E4 gets Linked devices with per-device revoke, plus a plain Log out. The vocabulary is already yours: sessions are just tokens you minted for yourself.

P1-09

Circle membership has no join flow, cap, or admin transfer

circlesgap

D2 lists members and offers Remove, but nothing specifies how someone becomes a member. Open the invite link and you're in? Then a forwarded circle link admits the whole building. There's also no member cap, no approval step, and no answer to what happens to a circle if the owner deletes their account — presumably it evaporates for everyone with no warning.

Fix

Join requires a display name and creates a bound holder credential (P0-01). Owner approval optional per circle, defaulting to on above ~10 members. Cap at 50 for v1. On owner deletion, warn explicitly that the circle dies, and offer admin transfer.

P1-10

Destructive circle actions skip the confirmation pattern the app already established

consistency

Revoking one token demands a confirmation sheet with a permanence warning. Disband circle — which cuts off four people at once — fires a toast. Removing a member is likewise a single tap. The app teaches a rule on one screen and breaks it on the next.

Fix

Route Disband through an F3-shaped sheet naming the member count: "4 people will lose access immediately. This cannot be undone." Member removal gets a lighter confirm, since it's recoverable by re-inviting.

03

P2 — craft gaps

Standard mobile behaviour that users will notice is missing.
P2-01

No swipe actions, long-press menus, or haptics

interaction
grep → swipe: 0 · contextmenu: 0 · vibrate: 0 · touchstart: 0

Every list in the app is tap-only. On mobile that reads as a web page wrapped in a shell — the single clearest tell that something isn't a native app.

Fix

Swipe on threads: left → Pause, right → Mute. Long-press a pass card for Copy link · Preview · Pause · Revoke. Haptics on mint success (notification), pause (light), and revoke confirmation (heavy) — never on revoke intent, only on completion.

P2-02

No search anywhere

scale

Fine at three tokens. At forty — the realistic state of a Pro user after a year — finding "the plumber from March" means scrolling a card list with no filter beyond three status buckets.

Fix

Pull-down search on Chats and Tokens, matching label, code and message body locally. Sort options on Tokens: recent, expiring soonest, most used.

P2-03

No pull-to-refresh and no reconciliation on foreground

sync

Token states change server-side — things expire while the app is closed. Nothing re-syncs on foreground, so a user can be looking at a token the server considers expired.

Fix

Reconcile on app foreground and on tab focus; pull-to-refresh on all four tabs. Expiry should be computed server-side and pushed, never inferred by the client.

P2-04

Chat lacks date separators, scroll-to-bottom, and pagination

messaging

Messages render as one unbroken run with time-only stamps. After a week the thread is unreadable. There's no jump-to-latest when scrolled up, and no windowing — a long thread renders every node.

Fix

Sticky date separators, a floating scroll-to-bottom with unread count, FlatList with inverted and windowing, and paged history.

P2-05

No keyboard avoidance, and Android back is unhandled

platform

Web prototypes get this free; React Native does not. The composer will sit under the keyboard on Android without explicit handling, and hardware back has no defined behaviour on sheets, modals, or the tab root — where it should exit rather than unwind history.

Fix

KeyboardAvoidingView with per-platform behaviour; test on a device with a gesture bar. Back closes the topmost sheet, then pops the stack, then exits from a tab root with the standard double-tap confirm.

P2-06

Cutting dark mode is a real cost, not a free simplification

designexpectation

Dropping the 4×2 theme system was right — trust products wear one uniform. But "one uniform" and "light only" aren't the same decision. A dark, security-flavoured app that blazes white at 11pm reads as unfinished, and reviewers say so.

Fix

One theme, two surfaces: the existing light palette plus a dark palette derived from the pass-card navy — which the brand already owns. Follow the system setting, no in-app picker. That preserves the discipline while meeting the expectation.

P2-07

No empty, loading, or error states on the holder page

web

The holder page has excellent semantic states — valid, paused, dead, business-gated — and no technical ones. If the API is down, a stranger on a 2G connection sees a blank page and concludes the link was a scam. That failure lands on the most fragile surface in the product.

Fix

Server-render the shell so something meaningful paints immediately. An explicit error state that reads as reassuring rather than broken: "This link is temporarily unavailable — try again in a moment." Aggressive performance budget: under 2s on 4G, under 5s on 3G.

P2-08

No crash reporting, analytics, or remote kill switch

operations

The spec names analytics events but nothing implements them, and there's no crash reporting or force-update mechanism. Shipping a solo-built app to a store with no telemetry means the first you hear of a crash loop is a one-star review.

Fix

Sentry or Crashlytics before the first external tester. A minimum-version check at launch with a blocking upgrade screen — you will need it. Feature flags so voice, circles and location can be dark-launched.

P2-09

Hindi is absent, and the copy is written for an English-first reader

localisationindia

Every string is hard-coded English, much of it idiomatic — "Perfectly silent", "that door no longer exists". For a India-first consumer product aimed partly at society groups and delivery interactions, that narrows the market considerably. The holder page matters more than the app here: the delivery agent opening the link is the least likely person in the chain to read English comfortably.

Fix

Externalise strings from day one — retrofitting is far worse. Ship the holder page in Hindi and English at minimum, auto-selected by Accept-Language. The app can follow.

04

P3 — polish

After the above.
P3-01

Clipboard is the default share path and it's leaky

privacy

Copied links sit in the system clipboard where other apps can read them; Android surfaces a toast, iOS shows a paste banner. Minor, but this product is judged by a higher standard.

Fix

Prefer the native share sheet over clipboard where possible, and clear the clipboard entry after a few minutes on Android.

P3-02

Toast and FAB occupy overlapping space

layout

The toast sits at bottom:104px, the FAB at bottom:16px with a 52px height. On a small screen with a tall toast they crowd, and the toast can obscure the primary action right after a mint.

Fix

Anchor the toast above the FAB with explicit spacing, or slide the FAB down while a toast is visible.

P3-03

The seal strip's tappability is under-signalled

affordance

It carries "Manage →" but otherwise reads as a status banner — the position WhatsApp uses for a non-interactive notice. Users may never discover it routes to Tokens.

Fix

Add a pressed state and a chevron. Worth measuring: if seal_strip_tapped stays near zero, it's decoration.

P3-04

No tablet or landscape handling

layout

Fixed phone-width assumptions throughout. Apple requires iPad screenshots only if you declare iPad support, so this is deferrable — but Android tablets and foldables will render awkwardly stretched.

Fix

Declare phone-only for v1. Add a max content width so foldables don't stretch lists to absurd line lengths.

07

The recommendation

How to get a real app into a real store fastest.

Ship v1 without voice

Voice drags in KYC, TRAI DLT, Exotel provisioning, CallKit and ConnectionService, and the entire E5 rejection-path UI. Most of that is paperwork you cannot parallelise or compress. Cut it, and the launch surface collapses to something one person can actually finish — while the product stays completely coherent, because the sentence still works: a link that lets someone reach you without your number, that you can kill at any time.

V1 — in
  • Mint, share, redeem, revoke, pause
  • Chat, with real delivery states
  • The holder web page, all states, Hindi + English
  • Location requests (no vendor dependency)
  • Circles, capped at 50
  • App lock, encrypted local store, recovery codes
  • Report → block → human triage
  • Privacy policy, terms, grievance officer, export, delete
V1.1 — deferred
  • Voice bridging and the Call channel
  • KYC (nothing left in v1 needs it)
  • SMS relay — DLT registration applies here too
  • Email relay
  • Paid tiers; keep v1 free while you learn the limits
  • Tablet and landscape

Keep the Call chip visible in the mint sheet, permanently in its locked state, with copy that tells the truth: "Voice calls are coming — chat and location work today." It sets the expectation, and it lets you measure how many people try to tap it, which tells you whether voice deserves to be the v1.1 headline.
