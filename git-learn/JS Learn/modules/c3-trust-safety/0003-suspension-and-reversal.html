Suspension, Reversal, and the Lever the User Already Has

C3 — Lesson 3 of 3  |  ~50 min

Why this matters for Token

Apple's Guideline 1.2 requires a response within 24 hours to reports of objectionable content. Declaring it is a condition of shipping. It is also a staffing commitment, and the staff is two people.

Twenty-four hours means twenty-four hours. It includes Saturday, it includes Diwali, and it includes the week one founder is on a plane with no signal. Two people cannot cover that by intention, and a rota does not create a third person.

This lesson is about what you do instead — and it opens with the good news, which is that Token's users already hold the strongest control in the system and do not need you at all to use it.

1 · Revocation is the lever, and it needs no operator

Every other product in this space handles abuse by asking the company to intervene. Token has something better, built into what the product is: the user can revoke.

Abuse arriving through a token ends the moment that token dies. No report, no queue, no 24-hour clock, no reviewer, no appeal. The person being bothered acts, and it is over — which is a better outcome than any moderation pipeline can offer, because it is instant and it does not require anybody to believe them.

Two properties make it work, and both are decided elsewhere and must not be quietly changed here:

  • Revoking ends contact and keeps the user's history (ADR-0009). The holder loses access and keeps no server-side copy; the user's record of the conversation survives. That matters directly for abuse: the evidence does not disappear when you stop the abuse.
  • A dead code says the same thing however it died (ADR-0010). Revoked, expired, exhausted, never-existed and account-deleted all produce one identical response. A holder who has been revoked cannot tell that they were specifically revoked, which is what stops the revocation itself becoming a message.

2 · The promise that cannot be kept by intention

Reporting still has to exist, because revocation only helps the person who is being contacted through a token they issued, and because both stores require a report mechanism regardless. Which brings back the 24 hours.

The failure here is not a people problem to be solved with discipline. It is arithmetic, and a declaration that rests on two diaries is a declaration that will be untrue on a specific, predictable day.

3 · Automate the protection, not the judgement

ADR-0011 splits the promise in half and automates exactly one side of it.

Everything that involves a judgement — is this actually harassment, was the context different, who is telling the truth — waits for a person in the morning. That part genuinely cannot be automated and pretending otherwise ends with a machine making findings about people.

The protective action does not wait. On a report in the serious categories, the reported party's access to that one conversation is suspended immediately, the reporter is told, and a human reviews it within the SLA and reverses it if it was wrong.

Why this half is the safe half to automate

Three properties, and it needs all three: the action is reversible, it is proportionate — one conversation, not the account — and it is exactly what a human would have done on reading the report anyway. Automating it changes the timing and nothing else.

The general form is worth keeping: the part of a promise that must never slip is the part that cannot depend on somebody being awake. This converts a staffing commitment into a system property, which is the only way a company of two makes a round-the-clock declaration honestly.

It is abusable, and that is not a hedge

A malicious reporter can silence somebody for a few hours by filing a serious report about them. That is a real harm to a real person and it will happen.

It is accepted because the alternative is a threat-of-violence report sitting unread overnight. Smaller, not zero — and the honest way to hold a decision like that is to measure the harm you accepted rather than to stop mentioning it.

4 · The instrumentation is the decision, not an add-on

Three numbers, required from day one:

  • How often it fires.
  • How often review reverses it. This is the measure of the harm above, and a high reversal rate is the evidence that would narrow the mechanism to threats and child safety only. It is written down as a measurement so that next time it is a number rather than an argument.
  • Who triggers it. A reporter whose suspensions are repeatedly reversed, against different people, is themselves the clearest abuse signal a system that cannot read content will ever produce.

That last one closes a loop with the previous lesson, and it is why the exercise below refuses to fire for such a reporter. Note the two distinctions it turns on, because both are the difference between a signal and a smear:

  • Reversed, not merely filed. A suspension a reviewer upheld is evidence the reporter was right. Counting it against them punishes people for reporting real abuse, which is the one thing this system cannot afford.
  • Distinct people, not reports. Three reversed reports about the same person is somebody who will not let go of one grievance. Three about three different people is a pattern.
Two figures get published, and the second one is the point

The transparency report carries suspensions applied and suspensions later reversed. Publishing the first alone reads as diligence. Publishing the reversal rate is what keeps the mechanism honest rather than convenient — it is the number that gets worse if you quietly widen the serious categories, and it is visible to everyone.

5 · Scope, and the drift to guard against

The suspension is scoped to one conversation. A holder suspended in one conversation is not banned from Token. Widening that to the account is a separate decision with a separate argument, and the thing to prevent is it happening by drift — one incident where an account-level block would have been convenient, and then a default that nobody ever decided.

The cheapest defence is that the function below has no way to express it. There is no account scope to pass, so widening the policy means changing the code and writing down why.
