Privacy Policy & Permissions

Privacy policy · Data safety · Permission strings · Store requirements ~40 min Reference
Why this matters for your WhatsApp clone

Both Google and Apple will reject your app without a privacy policy. Google also requires a "Data Safety" form explaining exactly what data you collect and why. Apple requires detailed permission usage descriptions — vague ones like "This app needs access" get rejected. This lesson gives you everything you need: a ready-to-use privacy policy template, the exact permission strings Apple approves, and Google's Data Safety form answers for a chat app.

1. What both stores require

Requirement Google Play App Store
Privacy policy URL Required — public URL Required — public URL
Data safety / App privacy Data Safety form in Play Console App Privacy section in App Store Connect
Permission descriptions Not shown to reviewers (but requested at runtime) Must explain WHY in Info.plist strings — reviewers read these
Account deletion Must offer in-app account deletion Must offer in-app account deletion
Age rating Content rating questionnaire Age rating questionnaire

2. Writing your privacy policy

Your privacy policy must explain: what data you collect, why, how it's stored, who can access it, and how users can delete their data. Here's a complete template for your WhatsApp clone:

<!-- privacy-policy.html — Host this on any public URL -->
<!DOCTYPE html>
<html lang="en">
<head>
  <meta charset="UTF-8">
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0">
  <title>Privacy Policy — WhatsApp Clone</title>
  <style>
    body { font-family: -apple-system, sans-serif; max-width: 700px; margin: 40px auto; padding: 0 20px; line-height: 1.6; color: #333; }
    h1 { color: #075E54; }
    h2 { color: #075E54; margin-top: 2rem; }
  </style>
</head>
<body>
  <h1>Privacy Policy</h1>
  <p><strong>Last updated:</strong> [DATE]</p>
  <p>[YOUR NAME] ("we", "our") operates the WhatsApp Clone mobile application. This policy explains how we collect, use, and protect your information.</p>

  <h2>1. Information We Collect</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Account information:</strong> Email address and display name (provided during registration)</li>
    <li><strong>Messages:</strong> Text messages, images, and voice messages you send through the app</li>
    <li><strong>Profile data:</strong> Profile picture and status text (optional)</li>
    <li><strong>Call data:</strong> Call history (caller, receiver, duration, timestamp) — NOT call audio/video content</li>
    <li><strong>Device tokens:</strong> Push notification tokens for delivering messages when the app is closed</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>2. How We Use Your Information</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>To deliver messages between you and other users</li>
    <li>To enable voice and video calls</li>
    <li>To send push notifications for new messages and incoming calls</li>
    <li>To display your profile to other users</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>3. Data Storage</h2>
  <p>Your data is stored in Firebase (operated by Google) on servers located in [REGION]. Messages are stored in Firestore. Media files (images, voice messages) are stored in Firebase Storage.</p>

  <h2>4. Data Sharing</h2>
  <p>We do NOT sell, rent, or share your personal data with third parties. Your messages are only visible to the intended recipients. We use the following services to operate the app:</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Firebase Authentication — for login</li>
    <li>Firebase Firestore — for message storage and delivery</li>
    <li>Firebase Cloud Messaging — for push notifications</li>
    <li>Firebase Storage — for media files</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>5. Voice & Video Calls</h2>
  <p>Calls use WebRTC peer-to-peer technology. Audio and video data flows directly between callers and is NOT recorded or stored on any server. Call metadata (who called whom, duration) is stored for your call history.</p>

  <h2>6. Your Rights</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Access:</strong> You can view all your data within the app</li>
    <li><strong>Deletion:</strong> You can delete your account and all associated data from Settings → Delete Account</li>
    <li><strong>Export:</strong> Contact us to request a copy of your data</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>7. Account Deletion</h2>
  <p>You can permanently delete your account from within the app (Settings → Delete Account). This removes: your profile, all messages you sent, your call history, and your media files. This action cannot be undone.</p>

  <h2>8. Children's Privacy</h2>
  <p>This app is not intended for children under 13. We do not knowingly collect data from children under 13.</p>

  <h2>9. Contact</h2>
  <p>Questions about this policy? Email: [YOUR_EMAIL]</p>
</body>
</html>

Replace the bracketed items: [DATE], [YOUR NAME], [REGION], [YOUR_EMAIL].

3. Where to host your privacy policy

Both stores need a publicly accessible URL. Free options:

Option How URL looks like

Firebase Hosting firebase deploy --only hosting yourapp.web.app/privacy
Notion Create a page, share publicly notion.so/yourname/Privacy-...
Google Sites Create free site, paste policy text sites.google.com/view/yourapp-privacy

Recommended: Firebase Hosting — you already have a Firebase project. Deploy the privacy policy HTML to it:

# In your project root:
mkdir -p web/privacy
# Save privacy-policy.html as web/privacy/index.html

firebase init hosting   # Select 'web' as public directory
firebase deploy --only hosting

# Your policy is now live at:
# https://your-project-id.web.app/privacy/

4. iOS permission usage descriptions

Apple requires you to explain why your app needs each permission in plain English. These strings appear in the system permission dialog. Vague descriptions get rejected. Here are the exact strings for your WhatsApp clone:

{
  "expo": {
    "ios": {
      "infoPlist": {
        "NSCameraUsageDescription": "WhatsApp Clone needs camera access to make video calls and take photos to send in chats.",
        "NSMicrophoneUsageDescription": "WhatsApp Clone needs microphone access to make voice and video calls, and to record voice messages.",
        "NSPhotoLibraryUsageDescription": "WhatsApp Clone needs photo library access to send existing photos and videos in chats.",
        "NSPhotoLibraryAddUsageDescription": "WhatsApp Clone needs permission to save photos and videos received in chats to your device.",
        "NSContactsUsageDescription": "WhatsApp Clone can access your contacts to help you find friends who also use the app."
      }
    }
  }
}

Apple's rules for permission strings:

  • Start with your app's name
  • Explain the specific feature that needs this permission
  • Never say just "This app requires access" — too vague
  • Don't include technical jargon — write for users, not developers
  • Keep it to 1–2 short sentences

5. Google Play Data Safety form

Google Play Console requires you to fill out a "Data Safety" form. For your WhatsApp clone, here's exactly what to answer:

Data collection:

Data type Collected? Shared? Purpose
Name Yes No App functionality (display name to other users)
Email address Yes No Account management (login)
Photos/Videos Yes No App functionality (sending media in chats)
Audio files Yes No App functionality (voice messages)
Messages Yes No App functionality (chat delivery)
Contacts Optional No App functionality (find friends)

Security practices:

  • Data encrypted in transit? — Yes (Firebase uses HTTPS/TLS for all communication)
  • Data can be deleted? — Yes (user can delete their account)
  • Independent security review? — No (not required for small apps)

6. Apple App Privacy labels

In App Store Connect, you fill out "App Privacy" with similar information. Apple shows this as a nutrition-label-style summary on your App Store listing:

Data Used to Track You:

None — your app doesn't track users across other apps.

Data Linked to You:

  • Contact Info (email)
  • User Content (messages, photos, audio)
  • Identifiers (user ID)

Data Not Linked to You:

  • Diagnostics (crash logs — if using Firebase Crashlytics)

Tip: Be honest. Apple and Google don't verify every detail, but if users report a discrepancy, your app can be removed. Better to over-disclose than under-disclose.

7. In-app account deletion (mandatory)

Both stores now require that users can delete their account from within the app. Here's how to implement it:

import { getAuth, deleteUser } from 'firebase/auth';
import {
  getFirestore, doc, deleteDoc, collection,
  query, where, getDocs, writeBatch
} from 'firebase/firestore';

async function deleteAccount() {
  const auth = getAuth();
  const db = getFirestore();
  const user = auth.currentUser;
  if (!user) return;

  const uid = user.uid;

  // 1. Delete user's Firestore data
  const batch = writeBatch(db);

  // Delete profile
  batch.delete(doc(db, 'users', uid));

  // Delete call history
  const callHistorySnap = await getDocs(collection(db, 'users', uid, 'callHistory'));
  callHistorySnap.forEach((d) => batch.delete(d.ref));

  // Delete user's messages (or mark as "deleted user")
  // Note: In a real app, you might anonymize rather than delete
  // because other users still see the conversation

  await batch.commit();

  // 2. Delete Firebase Auth account
  await deleteUser(user);

  // 3. The user is now logged out — navigate to login screen
}

Add this to your Settings screen:

function SettingsScreen({ navigation }) {
  async function handleDeleteAccount() {
    Alert.alert(
      'Delete Account',
      'This will permanently delete your account, messages, and all data. This cannot be undone.',
      [
        { text: 'Cancel', style: 'cancel' },
        {
          text: 'Delete',
          style: 'destructive',
          onPress: async () => {
            await deleteAccount();
            navigation.replace('Login');
          },
        },
      ]
    );
  }

  return (
    <View>
      {/* ... other settings ... */}
      <Pressable style={styles.deleteBtn} onPress={handleDeleteAccount}>
        <Text style={styles.deleteText}>Delete Account</Text>
      </Pressable>
    </View>
  );
}

Android permissions are declared in your app.json. Here's the complete list for your WhatsApp clone with explanations:

{
  "expo": {
    "android": {
      "permissions": [
        "android.permission.CAMERA",
        "android.permission.RECORD_AUDIO",
        "android.permission.READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE",
        "android.permission.WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE",
        "android.permission.POST_NOTIFICATIONS",
        "android.permission.USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT",
        "android.permission.FOREGROUND_SERVICE",
        "android.permission.VIBRATE",
        "android.permission.READ_CONTACTS"
      ]
    }
  }
}
Permission What it enables
CAMERA Video calls, taking photos
RECORD_AUDIO Voice/video calls, voice messages
READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE Selecting photos/videos to send
POST_NOTIFICATIONS Showing message and call notifications (Android 13+)
USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT Incoming call screen on lock screen
FOREGROUND_SERVICE Keep ringtone playing during incoming call
VIBRATE Vibrate for notifications and incoming calls
READ_CONTACTS Find friends (optional feature)

Important: Only request permissions you actually use. Both stores may question unnecessary permissions during review.

9. Requesting permissions at the right time

Don't ask for all permissions at once on first launch. Users deny blanket requests. Instead, ask in context — when the user tries to use the feature:

// ❌ Bad: Ask for everything on app launch
useEffect(() => {
  requestAllPermissions();  // User has no idea why — denies all
}, []);

// ✅ Good: Ask when the user taps the camera button
function handleTakePhoto() {
  const granted = await requestCameraPermission();
  if (granted) {
    openCamera();
  } else {
    Alert.alert(
      'Camera needed',
      'To take a photo, allow camera access in Settings.',
      [{ text: 'Open Settings', onPress: () => Linking.openSettings() }]
    );
  }
}

// ✅ Good: Ask for mic when they tap the call button
function handleStartCall() {
  const granted = await requestMicPermission();
  if (granted) {
    startCall();
  }
}

// ✅ Good: Ask for notifications after they send their first message
function handleFirstMessageSent() {
  Alert.alert(
    'Get notified',
    'Turn on notifications so you know when you get a reply.',
    [
      { text: 'Not now' },
      { text: 'Allow', onPress: () => requestNotificationPermission() },
    ]
  );
}

10. Age rating questionnaires

Google Play — Content Rating:

In Play Console, go to App content → Content rating and answer the IARC questionnaire. For a messaging app:

  • User-generated content: Yes (messages, photos)
  • Users can interact: Yes (chat, calls)
  • Users can share location: No (unless you added this)
  • Violence, gambling, etc.: No

Result: likely "Everyone" or "Teen" depending on your answers.

Apple — Age Rating:

In App Store Connect, answer the age rating questions:

  • Unrestricted Web Access: No
  • Gambling: No
  • Mature content: No
  • User-generated content: Yes

Result: likely 12+ (because of user-generated content / messaging).

11. Common rejection reasons (and how to avoid them)

Rejection reason Fix
"No privacy policy URL" Host your policy and enter the URL in both store dashboards
"Permission description too vague" (iOS) Be specific: "for video calls" not "this app needs access"
"No account deletion option" Add Delete Account in Settings (Section 7 above)
"Asks for permissions not used" Remove any permissions you don't actively use in code
"App requires login but no demo account" Provide a test account in the review notes (Apple requires this)
"Data Safety form doesn't match app behaviour" Re-check your form — make sure it lists ALL data you actually collect
Apple review tip: demo account

Since your app requires login, Apple reviewers need a way to test it. In App Store Connect → App Review Information, provide a demo email and password that the reviewer can use to log in. Create a test account with sample chats so the reviewer can see the app working.
