Intermediary Law & Content Liability

F1 — Lesson 2 of 16  |  ends in a document, not a quiz

Why this matters for Token

Token carries messages between people. That makes you an intermediary, and intermediaries get a specific bargain in Indian law: you are not liable for what your users say, provided you meet a list of duties. Miss the duties and the protection goes away — not for the message, for you.

There is a second reason this lesson exists, and it is the one people are unprepared for. Because messages are end-to-end encrypted, there are requests you genuinely cannot comply with. Not “will not”. Cannot. The time to write down exactly what you can and cannot produce is before a police officer is standing in front of you asking.

1 · Safe harbour, in your own words

The first task on the board says “summarise Section 79 safe harbour obligations in your own words”, and it is deliberate: if you cannot write the summary, you do not understand the deal you are relying on.

Here is the shape of it. Write your own version afterwards — the exercise is the point.

What you getWhat you must do to keep it
You are not liable for third-party content you transmit or hostLimit your role to providing the channel
Do not initiate the transmission, choose the receiver, or modify the content
Observe due diligence — the IT Rules duties below
Act on a valid order from a court or the appropriate government agency

Two consequences that matter for how you build and market Token:

  • “We do not read messages” is consistent with safe harbour, not in tension with it. Not initiating, not selecting the receiver and not modifying is exactly what an encrypted pipe does.
  • Editorialising costs you the protection. The moment you start choosing what gets delivered on grounds other than a rule the user set, you look less like a pipe. A user-configured rule — a time window, a per-day cap, text-only — is the user selecting, not you.

2 · Which IT Rules duties apply at your scale

The IT Rules 2021 have two tiers. The baseline applies to every intermediary. A much heavier set applies only to a significant social media intermediary — defined by a registered user threshold in India.

🔴 Confirm with counsel which tier you are in today. Almost certainly the baseline. Then do the thing nobody does:

Set the trigger before you need it

Write the user threshold into your metrics dashboard as an alert, at 50% of the number. The extra duties at that tier — among them a Chief Compliance Officer, a nodal contact person, a resident grievance officer, monthly compliance reports and a traceability requirement — take months to stand up and cost real money.

Crossing the line unknowingly is the failure mode. You do not get a letter; you are simply non-compliant from the day you crossed it. An alert at half the number buys you the runway to prepare, and costs one line in a dashboard.

Note also that the traceability requirement at that tier sits in genuine tension with end-to-end encryption. That is a live legal question and a business risk worth understanding before you approach the threshold, not after.

3 · Terms of service

⚖️ Counsel drafts these. Your job is the brief. Four things the brief must contain, because a lawyer cannot invent them:

a. The zero-tolerance clause — 🔴 Apple requires it explicitly

Apple’s review guideline for user-generated content requires terms that state there is no tolerance for objectionable content or abusive users. This is not a paraphrase you can approximate; reviewers look for it. Something in this shape:

There is no tolerance for objectionable content or abusive
behaviour on Token.

Content that is objectionable includes: threats of violence,
sexual content involving minors, harassment, hate speech
targeting a person or group, spam, and content that is illegal
under Indian law.

If you post such content or behave abusively toward another
person, we will remove your access. We may do this without
warning and without refund. We may also report you to the
authorities where the law requires it or where we judge that
someone is in danger.

Anyone using Token can report content or a person from inside
the app. We aim to act on reports within 24 hours.

b. Acceptable use, prohibited conduct, termination rights

Say what the product is for, what it is not for, and on what grounds access ends. Be specific to Token: issuing tokens to impersonate someone, harvesting redemption pages, automated enumeration of codes, and using a token to bypass a block you were previously given.

c. That a holder is bound too

People who redeem a token use the service without an account. They still need to be bound by rules of conduct, and the mechanism for binding them is different — there is no sign-up screen. That is lesson 0014; flag it in the brief so counsel drafts both sides at once rather than twice.

d. The limits of what the service can do

Terms are also where you say the honest, slightly unhelpful things: that revocation is irreversible, that nobody at the company can restore an account or read a message, and that a token already shared cannot be un-shared — only revoked. Users find these out anyway. Better they find out in a document than in a support thread at the worst moment.

4 · Law enforcement requests

This is the section to write properly, because it is the one that will be tested under pressure by someone with authority, at short notice, probably not during office hours.

The protocol

1. WHO RECEIVES IT
   One published address: legal@<domain>. Everything routes here,
   including things handed over in person or received by phone.
   Nobody else responds -- not support, not the co-founder, not
   in a chat thread.

2. IS IT VALID
   Check, and write down what you checked:
     - Is it in writing and on letterhead or an official channel?
     - Which authority, which officer, which case reference?
     - Which section of which law is being relied on?
     - Is it addressed to the right legal entity -- your company?
     - Is the scope specific -- named accounts, a date range --
       or is it a fishing request for everything?
   An oral request is not a request. Ask for it in writing.
   Asking is not obstruction.

3. WHAT WE CAN PRODUCE
   From the list below. Nothing outside the list, and nothing
   broader than the scope of the request.

4. WHO APPROVES
   Named person signs off before anything leaves. For anything
   unusual or urgent-sounding, counsel first. "Urgent" is the
   most common feature of an invalid request.

5. LOG IT
   Every request, valid or not, in the register. Date received,
   authority, scope, what we produced, who approved, date closed.
🔴 Document the recovery position in writing, before it is tested

Token’s messages are end-to-end encrypted. The server stores scrambled text and holds no key. A user’s key backup is protected by a recovery phrase that only the user has — deliberately, because a copy the company could release is a copy the company can be compelled to release, and at that point the encryption is decoration.

So when a valid order asks for message contents, the answer is not a refusal. It is “we do not have it, and here is why we cannot get it” — a technical fact about how the system is built, stated calmly, with the architecture document to back it up.

Write that explanation now, in one page, in language a non-engineer can follow, and have the technical founder confirm every sentence is true. Two things go wrong when it is improvised: you say something imprecise that later turns out to be false, or you sound evasive about a design decision you should be able to explain with confidence.

The two lists — write them out

What Token can produce on a valid requestWhat Token cannot produce, and why
Whether an account exists for a given identifierThe message contents — stored as ciphertext, no key held
Account creation date, display nameThe phone number itself — only a scrambled form is stored
Token metadata: labels, when issued, rules, state, when revokedA user’s encryption key — never sent to the server
That a redemption happened, and whenA recording of a call — there is no recording and nowhere to put one
Conversation timestamps and participants’ rolesThe network addresses of the parties on a call — not stored, deliberately
Holder-side details captured at redemption, within a specified scopeAnything older than your published retention window — it is gone
The right-hand column is a design commitment, not a policy

Two rows there are worth understanding because they are choices someone made and could unmake by accident.

No call recording, and nowhere to put one. The absence of a field is what makes the answer permanent. A product that has the column but leaves it empty has a different answer to give.

No network addresses from calls. Token relays every call through its own server precisely so that neither party learns the other’s address — that is the whole reason a delivery company cannot discover roughly where a user lives. If those addresses were then written into a call record, the policy would be defeated from the other side, in a table nobody thinks of as sensitive. Ask the technical founder to confirm, in writing, that they are not stored. Then keep that confirmation.

5 · What this section does not cover

Content moderation of the ordinary kind — scanning messages, classifying them, removing the bad ones — is not available to Token, because the server cannot read messages. That is not a gap in this lesson; it is a constraint the product chose.

What replaces it is a report pipeline: the person who can read the message — the recipient — packages it up and sends it to you, with enough cryptographic context to show it is genuine and not fabricated. That whole machine is lesson 0006, and it is what your UGC compliance evidence will actually consist of.

What this costs you

  • Counsel time on terms, and a slower brief than you expect because the holder side is unusual and has to be explained.
  • A permanent obligation to answer legal@ promptly, including on weekends. It is rarely used and cannot be ignored when it is.
  • Friction with a genuinely urgent request. There will be a day when someone with authority wants message contents in a hurry and the answer is that they do not exist. Having the one-page explanation ready turns that from an argument into a briefing. Not having it is where products make promises they cannot keep.
  • A ceiling on growth you should know about. The significant-intermediary tier brings duties that are in real tension with end-to-end encryption. That is a strategic problem, not a paperwork one, and it arrives with success rather than with failure.
