Registrations & Statutory Reporting

F1 — Lesson 12 of 16  |  ⌛ DLT registration is on the critical path of the entire launch

Login does not work without DLT registration

Token has no passwords. Sign-in is a one-time code sent by SMS. In India you cannot send a transactional SMS at all without being registered on a DLT platform, with a registered sender header and a pre-approved message template.

So the chain is: no DLT → no OTP → no sign-in → no closed test → no 12 testers for 14 days → no production access. A registration that takes one to three weeks sits underneath every other launch date in the project.

Note also what the earlier decision to drop phone-network calling did not change. Dropping PSTN voice removed the telecom licensing question for calls. It did not remove SMS DLT. Those are different regimes and only one of them went away.

1 · SMS DLT registration

Four steps, usually done through your SMS provider, who does this constantly and will guide you.

StepWhat it isWatch for
🔴 ⌛ Register as a principal entityYour company registers on a DLT platformCompany details must match your registration documents exactly
Register the sender headerThe six-character ID recipients see instead of a numberIt is part of your brand and it is hard to change. Pick it with lesson 0003 settled
Register the OTP templateThe exact wording, with variable placeholdersApproved wording is enforced. A message that does not match its template is rejected, silently, in production
Test delivery on all major operatorsSend real codes to real numbers on each networkDelivery differs by operator. One that fails will fail for a fifth of your users

The template — write it carefully, you are stuck with it

<#> {#var#} is your Token verification code. It expires in
10 minutes. Do not share it with anyone, including anyone
claiming to be from Token.
<hash>

Four things that draft is doing:

  • The variable is the only thing that changes. Everything else is fixed by the approval.
  • “Do not share it with anyone, including anyone claiming to be from Token.” This is the anti-phishing line, and it pairs with the rule from lesson 0008 that support must never ask for a code. The message and the policy have to agree, or one of them trains users to ignore the other.
  • It states the expiry. Users who know the code expires do not paste an old one and conclude the app is broken.
  • The markers at the start and end let the phone offer to autofill the code rather than making the user memorise it. Ask the technical founder to confirm the exact format for each platform — getting this right removes a real chunk of sign-up friction.

Fallbacks, and why they are not free

If SMS fails — a bad operator route, an outage, an aggregator problem — sign-in is down completely. The alternatives each have their own approval path and lead time:

FallbackReality
Voice OTP (a call reading the digits)Its own registration and template approval. Works where SMS routes are congested
OTP over a messaging platformA business-messaging approval process, template review, and a third party in your sign-in path. Weigh that against the product’s own principles
A second SMS aggregatorThe cleanest option, and it must be registered in advance — you cannot register one during an outage

Evaluate them now and pick one. The point of a fallback is that it exists before the day it is needed; a fallback with a three-week approval process, started on the day of the outage, is not a fallback.

Decided 2026-08-27 — a second aggregator, registered in advance

Two principal-entity registrations, two sender headers, two approved OTP templates, both live before launch. Not a plan to register a second one if the first fails — a second one already registered.

The whole argument is the lead time. DLT registration takes one to three weeks and you cannot start it during an outage, so the choice is not “one aggregator or two”. It is an outage of hours or an outage of weeks, decided in advance, by whether you did the paperwork twice.

Cost: a second set of registrations, a second header to choose, a second template to get approved and keep in step with the first, and a supplier relationship you may never use. Real effort for something you hope is waste.

Cost of the options rejected: a single aggregator is less work and means that when it fails, sign-in is down and no action is available to you — no new device can be added and nobody who logs out can get back in. Voice OTP as the fallback has its own registration path and, crucially, fails alongside SMS if the provider is the problem, which is the most likely way this breaks.

Two things that make it a real fallback rather than a filed form: keep the two templates identical in wording, so switching does not need a fresh approval; and send a live message through the standby on a schedule. An untested failover is a hypothesis. Put it on the compliance calendar in lesson 0011.

An OTP flow is a denial oracle by construction

“We sent you a code” versus “no such account” tells whoever is asking which numbers are registered with you. Indian mobile numbers have known prefixes and a small enough space to enumerate completely, so that difference is not a small leak — it is a downloadable list of your users.

The response must be identical either way: same words, same status, same timing. This is a rule the product has had to apply at several layers already, and the OTP screen is the one that faces the whole internet.

It also has a cost you should know about: a user who mistypes their number gets a code that never arrives and no explanation. That is the accepted trade. Handle it in the interface — show the number back for confirmation before sending — not by telling the attacker.

2 · CERT-In directions 🔴

A separate regime from DPDP, with a different recipient, a different clock and a different threshold. Do not merge it with the DPDP breach runbook. They look similar and combining them produces a process that is late for one and over-reports to the other.

DPDP breach notificationCERT-In incident reporting
RecipientThe Data Protection Board, and affected usersCERT-In
ClockThe DPDP timeline6 hours from noticing
TriggerA personal data breachA listed cyber incident — broader than a data breach
FormatAs prescribed under DPDPCERT-In’s format and channel

The six-hour workflow

WHAT QUALIFIES
  Read the list of reportable incident types and write your
  own version. It is broader than "user data leaked" -- it
  includes things like unauthorised access to systems, a
  compromised account, and certain attacks on your
  infrastructure. Some things that are NOT a data breach are
  still reportable.

WHO NOTICES
  Same detection routes as the DPDP runbook. This is the one
  place the two processes share a step.

WHO REPORTS
  One named person, one named deputy. Both know the channel
  and have used it once in a drill.

TO WHERE, IN WHAT FORMAT
  The address and the format, written down, before you need
  it. Do not plan to look it up inside a six-hour window.

THE CLOCK
  Six hours from NOTICING, not from the incident starting,
  and not from understanding it. You will be reporting an
  incomplete picture. That is expected -- report on time and
  supplement afterwards.
Six hours means you report before you understand

This is the part founders get wrong. The instinct is to investigate first and report a clear account. Six hours does not allow that, and lateness is the violation — incompleteness is not.

So prepare the report as a form with blanks, and accept that the first submission will say “scope under investigation” in several fields. Practise it in the quarterly drill from lesson 0001, with a timer running.

Two technical confirmations you need from the technical founder

  • Log retention. There is a requirement to retain certain logs for 180 days. Confirm which logs, where they are kept, and that the retention is actually configured — and reconcile it with your data-minimisation instincts and your published retention promises. These can conflict: a promise to delete quickly and an obligation to retain for 180 days both apply, to different things. Write down which is which, per log, and get counsel’s view where they overlap.
  • Clock synchronisation. System clocks must be synchronised to the specified Indian time sources. This sounds trivial and it is the thing that makes your logs admissible and comparable with someone else’s. Confirm it is configured on every server, not just the main one.

3 · Regulatory watch 🔁

Three things to track, each with a real reason and a real consequence.

WhatWhy it matters to TokenWhat to watch for
DPDP RulesThe Act is passed; the Rules put numbers on the obligations. Timelines, thresholds and formats firm up when they landNotification of the Rules, and any transition period. Your policies will need a pass
TRAI’s OTT consultationToken is exactly the category under discussion. An app carrying messages and calls over the internetAny move toward licensing, KYC for users, traceability, or interception obligations for OTT communication services
CERT-In directionsThey are updated, and the obligations are operational rather than policyChanges to reportable types, retention, or the reporting channel
Two thresholds to note now, so crossing them is not a surprise
  • Significant Data Fiduciary. Under DPDP the government can designate a fiduciary as significant, based on volume and sensitivity of data and other factors. The designation brings extra duties — a Data Protection Officer in India, independent audits, impact assessments. It is not automatic on a number, but volume is a factor, so it arrives with growth.
  • Significant social media intermediary. The user threshold from lesson 0002. The traceability duties at that tier sit in genuine tension with end-to-end encryption.

Both are consequences of success. Put both on the metrics dashboard as alerts at half the relevant number, so the preparation window exists.

What this costs you

  • 1–3 weeks of lead time on the critical path. The single most schedule-critical item in this module after the 14-day test it blocks.
  • A per-message SMS cost, forever, that scales with sign-ins. Rate limiting is a cost control as much as a security control.
  • A six-hour clock you must be able to meet at 3am on a Sunday, which means a prepared form and a rehearsed channel rather than good intentions.
  • A retention obligation that may pull against your privacy promises. Some logs must be kept for 180 days. Reconcile it explicitly rather than discovering the conflict during an incident.
  • A standing regulatory watch in a space where the rules for exactly this category are actively being written.
