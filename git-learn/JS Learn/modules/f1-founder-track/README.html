Founder Track — the non-technical half of shipping Token

Track F

Phase 7 — 16 lessons  |  no quizzes — every lesson ends in a document you write

Module goal

Everything required to publish, operate and commercialise Token that is not engineering: data protection, store compliance, trust and safety, positioning, support, vendors, registrations, and the security of your own accounts. It assumes company incorporation is already handled.

These lessons are the counterpart to the source task board at token-nontech-taskboard.md. The board is the checklist; this module is the why, the worked example, and the shape of the finished artefact.

Why this module has no quiz

Every other module in this course ends in a quiz, because the thing being taught is a behaviour a computer can check — does this code run, does this key match. Nothing here is like that. The deliverable of every lesson below is a document, a decision or a registration, and there is no multiple-choice question that can tell you whether your Record of Processing Activities matches your actual database.

So each lesson ends with a write-it-down box instead. What you type is saved in your browser and is still there next time. That box is the assessment: if you cannot fill it in, the lesson has not landed.

This is not legal advice

It is a founder’s working brief. Anything marked ⚖️ needs a professional — a lawyer, a company secretary, a chartered accountant. Your job for those items is to prepare and brief well, so the professional spends their hours on judgement rather than on discovering what your product does. A well-prepared brief is the difference between a two-week turnaround and a two-month one.

Read the two longest chains together and the schedule falls out of them. The RoPA is upstream of four separate deliverables, and DLT registration is upstream of the fourteen-day test that is upstream of production access. Those two are the critical path. Everything else has slack.

The six that would actually stop launch

If nothing else in this module gets done, these do.

  1. Play’s 12 testers for 14 days — pure calendar time. There is no version of working harder that makes fourteen days into seven, and a tester dropping out restarts the count.
  2. Data inventory — both stores’ privacy forms are generated from it, and a mismatch between what you declared and what the app does is a rejection on both stores and a DPDP problem on top.
  3. UGC compliance evidence — content filtering, in-app reporting, blocking abusive users, a published contact. Missing any one of the four is an automatic rejection on both stores.
  4. Web-accessible account deletion — Play requires a URL a person can reach without installing the app. An in-app path alone does not satisfy it.
  5. A named, published grievance officer — statutory, not optional, and it needs a real address as well as an email.
  6. SMS DLT registration — login OTPs do not deliver without it, which blocks the closed test, which blocks the fourteen days, which blocks production access.
One contradiction you will hit, flagged deliberately

The source task board names LiveKit and AWS Mumbai in its vendor list. The architecture this course teaches uses self-hosted coturn for call relay and a Coolify VPS for hosting, with no third-party communications SDK at all. Those cannot both be true.

Nothing in this module resolves that for you — it is an architecture decision, not a compliance one. But it changes your vendor list, your data-processing agreements and your residency answers, so settle it with the technical founder before you sign a single DPA. Lesson 0009 is written against the roles (“whoever relays your calls”) rather than the names, so it survives either answer.

How to work through this module

  • Do the six start-now items first, out of order. They are waiting on other people. Everything else is waiting on you, and you are faster.
  • One lesson, one artefact. Do not read three lessons and then start writing. The write-it-down box at the foot of each lesson is the unit of work.
  • Keep the artefacts in one place — a folder, a shared drive, a repo. Half of them are things you will have to hand to a lawyer, a store reviewer or a regulator, and “it is in a chat thread somewhere” is how a two-day job becomes a two-week one.
  • Date every document and record who decided. Compliance is not the document; it is being able to show what you decided, when, and on what basis.
