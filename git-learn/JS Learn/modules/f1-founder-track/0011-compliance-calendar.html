The Compliance Calendar

F1 — Lesson 11 of 16  |  the shortest lesson, and the one that keeps the other fifteen alive

Why this matters for Token

Every other lesson in this module ends with at least one 🔁 — a recurring duty. Individually each is small. Collectively they are a job, and the way that job fails is not dramatic: nothing happens. No alert fires when a quarterly policy review does not occur. The threat catalogue simply sits there, describing a product from a year ago, and nobody notices until something it should have covered goes wrong.

This lesson is one artefact: a calendar with owners and dates. It is the mechanism that makes the other fifteen lessons still true in a year.

2 · Turning a list into a calendar

Four rules. They are what separate a calendar that works from a document that is admired once.

  1. Every entry has one named owner. Not “the founders”. A duty owned by two people is owned by neither, and this failure is worse in a two-person company than in a large one, because both of you are always busy with something urgent.
  2. Every entry has a date, not a frequency. “Quarterly” is a wish. “5 January, 5 April, 5 July, 5 October” is a calendar entry that appears on a phone.
  3. Every entry names its artefact. What exists afterwards that did not exist before? A quarterly policy review produces a dated note saying what was reviewed and what changed — including “nothing changed”, which is a valid and useful outcome recorded.
  4. The continuous ones need a trigger, not a slot. You cannot put “monitor store policy changes” on a Tuesday. Subscribe to the announcement feed and route it somewhere you read. A continuous duty without a mechanism is a duty you have not assigned.

A worked entry

DUTY      Quarterly policy review
OWNER     <name>
DATES     5 Jan, 5 Apr, 5 Jul, 5 Oct
INPUTS    privacy policy, terms, retention policy, the RoPA,
          the changelog since the last review
ARTEFACT  a dated note: what was reviewed, what changed,
          what was decided not to change and why
TAKES     about 90 minutes
FAILS IF  a feature shipped in the quarter touched data and
          the RoPA was not updated at the time

That last line is worth copying into every entry. Naming the failure mode is what turns a review from a ritual into a check. If you cannot say what would make this review find something, it will find nothing.

3 · The monthly review of the calendar itself

Fifteen minutes, once a month, and it is the entry that keeps the rest honest.

  • What did not happen last month? Not to assign blame — to find out whether it was a bad month or a badly designed duty. A quarterly task that has been missed twice is not a discipline problem; it is either not important or not owned.
  • What is new? A feature that shipped, a vendor that was added, a regulation that changed. Each may create a duty.
  • What is obsolete? Duties for features you removed. Delete them. A calendar with dead entries stops being read, exactly the way an alert that cries wolf stops being read — and then a real one is missed inside the noise.
  • Is the owner still right? Roles drift.

4 · Where to keep it

Two artefacts, not one.

ArtefactPurposeRule
The calendar entriesMaking the thing happenIn a real calendar, with reminders, on both founders’ phones. Not in a document
The registerProving it happenedOne page per duty: date done, who, what changed, link to the artefact

The register is the half people skip, and it is the half that matters when someone asks. The answer to “do you review your privacy policy?” is not “yes”; it is a dated list of the last four reviews. Compliance is rarely about the document. It is about being able to show what you did, when, and on what basis.

One duty per line, one home per fact

Resist the urge to also keep status in the task board, in a spreadsheet and in a chat thread. A fact with three homes has three chances to be wrong, and the version people quote is whichever one they saw last.

The calendar makes it happen; the register records that it happened. Nothing else tracks it.

What this costs you

  • Roughly two days a month once everything is running, most of it in the daily triage and support duties that belong to lessons 0006 and 0008 rather than here.
  • An hour to build it, and fifteen minutes a month to keep it alive.
  • The discipline of recording “nothing changed”, which feels pointless and is the entire evidentiary value of the register.
