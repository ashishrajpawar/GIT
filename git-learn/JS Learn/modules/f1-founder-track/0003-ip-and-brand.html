IP & Brand

F1 — Lesson 3 of 16  |  ⌛ the naming decision has a four-week clock on it

Why this matters for Token

This lesson exists because of one uncomfortable possibility: the product may be called the wrong thing.

“Token” is close to generic in its own product class — the thing the app issues is literally called a token — and generic marks are hard or impossible to register. On top of that, one documented comprehension failure has already happened: a third-party reviewer came away believing Token was a queue-management app, which is what “token” means to a large number of Indian users standing in a bank.

Neither problem gets cheaper with time. Every week you keep the name, more of your brand equity is attached to it — the domain, the handles, the store listing, the press coverage, the users who learned the word. Make the decision by week four.

1 · The trademark search

⌛ Start this now; it informs the deadline above.

ClassCoversWhy Token needs it
9Downloadable software, mobile applicationsThe app itself
38Telecommunications, transmission of messagesCarrying messages and calls
42Software as a service, hostingThe backend and the redemption web page

Search all three. A mark is registered per class, so a clear result in one says nothing about the others, and a communications app that owns class 9 and not class 38 owns half of what it does.

What you are looking for, in order of severity:

  1. An identical or near-identical mark already registered in one of your classes. This is a stop sign, not a negotiation.
  2. A confusingly similar mark — similar sound, similar meaning, similar look. “Confusing” is judged from the point of view of an ordinary consumer, not a lawyer.
  3. A pending application filed before yours. Priority runs from the filing date.
  4. Nothing registered, but the word is in common descriptive use in the class. This is the Token problem, and it is the subtle one.

2 · Registrability — the actual issue

Trademark law places marks on a spectrum. Roughly, strongest to weakest:

KindExample shapeRegistrable?
InventedA word that did not exist before you coined itStrongest. Register easily, defend easily
ArbitraryA real word with no relation to the productVery strong
SuggestiveHints at a benefit without describing itRegistrable, moderately strong
DescriptiveDescribes what the product is or doesWeak. Needs acquired distinctiveness — years of use and evidence
GenericIs the common name for the thing itselfNot registrable, at all, ever

“Token”, for an app whose core object is called a token, sits at the wrong end of that table. You may be told it is descriptive rather than fully generic, which means registrable only after long use and evidence you do not yet have.

The risk of building on an unregisterable mark

It is not that someone sues you. It is that you cannot stop anyone else.

Without a registration you have very little to send to an app store when a lookalike app appears, very little to send to a registrar when someone puts up a clone of your redemption page on a similar domain, and very little to send to a platform when an impersonation account starts collecting your users’ details. Lesson 0015 lists those takedown routes and every one of them works better with a registration behind it.

For a privacy product this is worse than average, because a convincing clone of your redemption page is the predictable attack on this design. Your name is the only thing a user has to check.

⚖️ Get a trademark attorney’s written view. Ask three specific questions, not “can we register this?”:

  1. Is this mark registrable in classes 9, 38 and 42 as it stands, and if not, what is the obstacle in each?
  2. Would a composite — the word plus a distinctive logo, or the word plus a second word — be registrable, and what would we then actually own?
  3. If we launch without a registration, what enforcement options do we have against a clone or an impersonating app, and how much weaker are they?

3 · The naming decision

By week four. Three outcomes are legitimate; drifting is not.

OptionWhat it buysWhat it costs
Keep the nameNo rework. The word describes the mechanic accurately to anyone who has understood the product onceWeak or no registration. The queue-management misreading persists and must be beaten by the first screen instead of the name. Enforcement against clones is hard
Composite mark — keep the word, register the word-plus-logoSomething registrable, no rename, some enforcement footingYou own the composite, not the word. A competitor may still use the word
RenameA registrable, distinctive mark; a chance to fix the comprehension problem at the same timeDomain, handles, store reservations, every asset, and any recognition already built. Cheap now, expensive in six months, ruinous after launch
Do not merge this with the comprehension problem — but do decide them together

They are different failures. The name is a legal problem about ownership; the review that concluded Token was a queue app is a positioning problem about the first screen and the missing premise. A rename does not automatically fix comprehension, and a better first screen does not make the mark registrable.

But they share a deadline, because if you are going to rename at all, the new name should be chosen with the comprehension test in hand. Do lesson 0007 before you finalise, and take any candidate name through the same ten-stranger test.

4 · Securing the pieces

Domain

  • The primary domain, registered to the company, not to a founder’s personal account.
  • Defensive variants: the obvious misspellings, the hyphenated form, the common alternate TLDs, and the singular/plural pair. You are not building a portfolio — you are denying the five most convincing clone addresses.
  • Lock it against transfer, turn on registry lock if the registrar offers it, and set the expiry to auto-renew on a card that does not expire before the domain does. Losing the domain of a product whose redemption page is a URL is not recoverable by apology.

Social handles

Reserve the handle everywhere you might ever post, plus the places you never will — those are the ones an impersonator uses. Same name, same spelling, everywhere. A user checking whether an account is really you should not have to remember that you are one thing on one platform and something else on another.

Store names — first come, first served

Reserve the app name on both stores as soon as the developer accounts exist. App name reservation is not part of trademark law and does not wait for it. It is a queue, and the queue does not care that you had the idea first. Lessons 0004 and 0005 are ⌛ for account creation partly for this reason.

5 · Ownership inside the company

Confirm IP assignment from both founders to the company

By default, in many arrangements, the person who writes the code or designs the logo owns the copyright in it — not the company they founded. If that is never fixed, the company does not actually own its own product.

It surfaces at exactly two moments, both bad: due diligence for an investment, and a founder leaving. Fix it with a written assignment now, while everyone agrees. It is a short document and a company secretary or lawyer does it routinely.

Cover: source code, designs, the logo and brand assets, the domain, documentation, and anything created before incorporation that is now part of the product.

Third-party assets — check every licence

The student’s standing constraint on this project is that everything must be open source: no commercial licences, no paid SDKs. Rented hardware is fine; software is not. That makes this check mostly a confirmation exercise — but “free to download” and “free to ship in a commercial app” are different things, and fonts and icons are where the difference bites.

AssetWhat to checkThe common trap
FontsLicence permits embedding in an app and use in store screenshotsA free-for-personal-use font on the marketing site
IconsLicence, and whether attribution is requiredAttribution required and never given — a real licence breach
LibrariesThe licence of each dependency, including transitive onesA copyleft licence deep in the tree that reaches your whole app
Illustrations, stock imagesCommercial use, and use in store listings specificallyStore screenshots count as advertising under some stock licences
Sounds — ringtone, notificationSame as aboveRingtones are almost always licensed, rarely free

Produce one file listing every third-party asset, its licence, and where the licence text lives. It takes an afternoon, it is required for the app’s open-source notices anyway, and it is the first thing an acquirer or investor asks for.

Copyright on brand assets

Copyright exists automatically; registration is optional in India and mainly buys you easier evidence in a dispute. Worth doing for the logo if the mark itself turns out not to be registrable — in that scenario the logo may be the strongest thing you own.

What this costs you

  • Attorney fees for search and opinion, then filing fees per class if you proceed — three classes means three filings.
  • A real chance of a rename, and everything that touches. This is why the deadline is four weeks rather than “before launch”: the cost of the same decision grows every week you defer it.
  • Registration takes months to years. You will launch with an application pending, not a registration in hand. Plan enforcement on that basis.
  • A small annual bill for defensive domains, forever, and renewal dates that must go on the compliance calendar in lesson 0011.
