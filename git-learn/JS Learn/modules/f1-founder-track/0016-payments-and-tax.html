Payments & Tax

F1 — Lesson 16 of 16  |  parked, not deleted — and the parking has an expiry

Why this is parked, and why it is still here

Payments are out of the current scope by decision: unit economics wait for the final architecture, and there is no point pricing a product whose infrastructure cost is not known. The database models subscription tiers; no gateway is integrated.

It is kept as a lesson rather than deleted for one reason: two items in it have lead times measured in weeks, and both are the kind of thing that gets discovered on the day the paid tier is otherwise ready. Know-your-business verification with a payment gateway, and the GST position, are not same-day jobs.

So this lesson is a trigger, not a task list. When the paid tier moves from “someday” to “this quarter”, this whole lesson activates and the items below start about six to eight weeks before you intend to charge anybody.

Store billing versus your own gateway — decide this first

It determines everything below it, and for a subscription inside a mobile app the answer is largely dictated by store policy rather than by preference.

Store billingYour own gateway
CommissionA significant percentage of every paymentMuch lower per-transaction cost
Compliance workThe store handles receipts, refunds, mandates and tax in many casesAll of it is yours: KYB, invoicing, mandates, refunds
Store policyRequired for digital goods consumed in the app, in most casesPermitted for some categories and in some jurisdictions, with conditions
User experienceOne tap, already trusted, already has a card on fileMore friction, more drop-off
DataYou learn less about the payer — which for a privacy product is a genuine advantageYou hold payment relationships and the obligations that come with them
Decided 2026-08-27 — store billing for the first paid tier

The direction is set even though the work is parked: the first paid tier goes through Play Billing and Apple’s in-app purchase, not through your own gateway.

What it buys: the store handles receipts, refunds, renewal reminders and much of the tax treatment; RBI e-mandate complexity for recurring charges is theirs rather than yours; and you learn less about the payer, which for this product is a feature rather than a compromise.

Cost, and it is the large one: a significant commission on every payment, permanently, which changes what a subscription has to be priced at to work.

Cost of the option rejected: your own gateway is much cheaper per transaction. It also brings a KYB review of your business, compliant invoicing, GST, e-mandate rules and refunds — all yours — plus billing names, payment identifiers and transaction history in your database. That would be the largest single expansion of what you hold about a user in the product’s history, for a product built on holding almost none.

What this changes today: almost nothing, except that when the trigger in §1 fires you skip the gateway-selection and KYB rows entirely and go straight to store billing setup. Send the CA letter about GST anyway — store billing simplifies the treatment, it does not remove the question.

What would reverse it: commission at scale making the unit economics impossible, or a pricing model the stores will not support. Both are reasons to revisit; neither is knowable until there is revenue, which is exactly why this stays parked.

The privacy angle is not a footnote here

Every payment method you operate yourself adds a row to the RoPA in lesson 0001 — billing name, payment identifiers, transaction history — and every one of those rows appears on both store privacy forms, under Financial info.

For a product built on collecting almost nothing, taking payments is the single largest expansion of what you hold about a user that will ever happen. Whichever route you choose, the data question belongs in the decision, alongside the commission. Store billing means the store knows and you do not; that is worth real money in commission and it is also the more consistent answer for this product.

3 · What to prepare now, while it is parked

Three things cost almost nothing today and remove weeks later:

  1. Ask your CA the GST question once, in writing, now. Whether a subscription to your service is taxable, at what rate, what the place-of-supply rules mean for users outside your state or country, and what registration threshold applies. The answer is stable, and having it in writing turns a later scramble into a form-filling exercise.
  2. Keep the subscription tiers in the schema honest. They are already modelled. Make sure the model can express what you would actually sell — the trap is discovering that your pricing idea needs a schema change during the sprint you meant to integrate a gateway.
  3. Keep the refund policy drafted and unpublished. Lesson 0013 already wrote it. Publish it the day you charge.

4 · The things that will surprise you

  • Recurring payments in India have their own mandate rules. Automatic renewal is not simply a matter of storing a card; there are notification and authorisation requirements around e-mandates, and they shape the user experience as well as the integration. This is a reason to prefer store billing for a first paid tier.
  • KYB is a review of your business, not a form. Gateways ask what you sell, to whom, and may look at your app. A communications product with encryption is unusual enough to attract questions. Answer them with the same one-page explanation you wrote for law enforcement in lesson 0002 — it is the same material.
  • Refunds are a consumer-protection matter as well as a payments one. The policy from lesson 0013 and the gateway’s refund mechanism have to agree, and the store has its own refund flow on top.
  • “No in-app purchases” is a declaration you already made on both stores. Changing it means updating the listings, the Data Safety form and the privacy labels — a small task that people forget until a reviewer catches the inconsistency.

What this costs you

  • Nothing today, which is the point of parking it — except one letter to your CA.
  • 6–8 weeks of lead time whenever it activates, mostly KYB and GST. Not compressible.
  • A commission on every payment if you take store billing, in exchange for most of the compliance work and less data about your users.
  • The largest single expansion of the data you hold, for a product whose premise is holding almost none. That is a real cost and it deserves to be weighed alongside the commission rather than after it.
