Apple App Store

F1 — Lesson 5 of 16  |  ⌛ enrolment needs a D-U-N-S number

Why this matters for Token

Apple asks for the same four UGC guarantees as Google and one extra promise: a 24-hour response to reports of objectionable content. That is not a document. It is a staffing commitment, and if it is not genuinely staffed you have declared something untrue to a reviewer who may test it.

The second thing Apple gets right, from your point of view, is that it makes you say the same things twice — privacy nutrition labels here, Data Safety on Play — and a reviewer on either side can read both. They must tell the same story. A different answer on each store is not two forms; it is evidence that at least one of them is wrong.

The D-U-N-S number is the hidden wait

An organisation enrolment cannot proceed without one, it is issued by a third party, and the details on it must match your company registration exactly — legal name, address, everything. A mismatch is a rejection followed by another wait.

Check whether your company already has one before requesting a new one. Duplicates cause their own delay.

Declaring end-to-end encryption to a store is a commitment

Both stores let you say your messages are end-to-end encrypted, and for Token that is a headline claim. It also means you have told two platform operators something checkable.

So keep the claim exactly as narrow as the design. Token is end-to-end encrypted against a server that stores and does not attack: the server holds only ciphertext and no key. It is not protected against a server that actively substitutes a key in its own directory, because nothing surfaces that substitution to the user — a deliberate product decision, on the grounds that a cryptographic warning shown to someone messaging a delivery driver is noise they learn to dismiss.

That is a real and common posture and it is not a false declaration: the data genuinely is end-to-end encrypted. What it means for your copy is a rule you can apply mechanically — never write “nobody can intercept your messages”; do write “we store only scrambled text and hold no key.” The first is a claim about the whole world. The second is a claim about your own systems, and it is one you can keep.

3 · Guideline 1.2 — user-generated content 🔴

Apple’s four requirements are the same four as Play, plus a response-time promise:

  1. A method for filtering objectionable content. Same answer as Play, and it needs the same careful wording — what you do instead of server-side scanning, not the fact that you cannot scan. See lesson 0004 §3.
  2. A mechanism to report offensive content, with a response within 24 hours.
  3. The ability to block abusive users.
  4. Published contact information so users can reach you.

And, from the terms side, the zero-tolerance clause written in lesson 0002. Apple asks for it explicitly.

Confirm the 24-hour SLA is genuinely staffed

Twenty-four hours means twenty-four hours, including Saturday, including Diwali, including the week one founder is on a plane. Two people cannot cover that by good intentions.

Before you declare it, answer these four in writing — they belong in lesson 0006 and this is the deadline that forces them:

  • Who is on duty by default, and who is the named backup?
  • What wakes them — an email alert, a phone notification, a shared inbox rule? “Someone checks the inbox” is not a mechanism.
  • What is the minimum acceptable action inside 24 hours? Usually: acknowledge, assess, and take an interim protective action. A full investigation is not required in a day; a response is.
  • What happens on the day both of you are unreachable?

The honest fallback for a two-person company is an interim action that is safe to take automatically — suspending the reported party’s access to that conversation pending review — so that the 24-hour promise is kept by a mechanism rather than by a person being awake.

4 · Guideline 5.1.1 — data collection and storage

Read it properly; it is the guideline most likely to catch a product like this one. The parts that matter for Token:

  • Ask for permission at the moment you need it, with a clear purpose string. Camera permission should be requested when the user opens the QR scanner, not at launch.
  • Do not require data the app does not need to function. Token asks for a phone number and a display name. That is defensible; anything more would need a reason.
  • Sign-in must not be required to use functionality that does not need an account. This is directly relevant: the redemption page must work for a holder with no account. It already does — make sure the reviewer sees that.
  • Account deletion must be available in the app if account creation is. Apple requires the in-app path; Google requires the web path. You need both.

6 · Assets, description and rating

  • Screenshots for every required device class you support. Apple is stricter than Play about sizes, and a missing size blocks submission rather than warning you.
  • Description — the same one-paragraph version as Play, adapted. Consistency across stores is worth more than optimisation on either.
  • The keyword field — comma-separated, tight character limit, not shown to users. This is where the queue-management misreading can actually be used to your advantage or actively hurt you. Include the words people would search when they have the problem: privacy, phone number, spam calls, temporary number, share safely. Do not include competitor names.
  • Age rating — answer the same way you answered IARC on Play, and make both match the minimum age in your terms.

What this costs you

  • An annual fee, indefinitely, and a lapse pulls the app from sale.
  • 1–3 weeks of enrolment, longer if the D-U-N-S details do not match.
  • A staffing promise you cannot walk back. The 24-hour SLA is the single most operationally expensive line in this whole module for a two-person company.
  • Duplicated declaration work that must stay in sync forever — every feature that touches data now updates two forms, not one.
  • A stricter, slower review than Play, with a human on the other end who can and will ask questions.
