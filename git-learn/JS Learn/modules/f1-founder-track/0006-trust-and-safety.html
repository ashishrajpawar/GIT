Trust & Safety Operations

F1 — Lesson 6 of 16  |  this is the lesson both stores audit

Why this matters for Token

Two stores require four things: filtering, reporting, blocking, a published contact. This lesson is where three of the four actually get built, and where the evidence for all four comes from.

It is also where a specific defect gets fixed. A review of the prototype found that the report button sent nothing anywhere — it showed a confirmation to the person reporting and stopped. That is the worst possible failure mode: it is invisible to the user, it satisfies a demo, and it means every report ever made was discarded. Assume nothing about the pipeline until you have watched a test report arrive.

1 · The threat model — catalogue what is specific to Token

Generic abuse lists are useless here, because Token’s design removes some categories and creates others. Write your own catalogue. A starting set:

AbuseHow it worksMitigated?
Forwarded linkThe holder passes the redemption URL to someone else. The token was issued to one party and is now used by anotherPartly. A use cap and an expiry bound it; nothing proves who is at the other end
Second-device accessThe holder opens the same conversation on another device or hands the page to a colleagueWeakly. Inherent to a browser-based holder with no account
Code enumerationAn attacker guesses codes to find live tokensYes, if rate limiting is right — and the code space is enormous. Verify it, do not assume it
Harassment inside a legitimate tokenA person the user genuinely gave a token to abuses the channelThis is the main one. Report, block, revoke
Listing tokens as an attack surfaceA token published somewhere public is redeemed by strangers rather than the intended partyPartly. Use caps and rules. Also a user-education problem
Impersonation of Token itselfA cloned redemption page harvests what a holder typesLesson 0015. This is the predictable attack on this design
Unwanted contact by rule evasionContacting at the edge of a rule — ten messages in ten seconds under a daily cap of tenOpen. A per-minute cooldown was considered and deferred; know that the gap exists

For each row, mark mitigated in the current build or open. The open ones are not failures; they are things you have decided to accept for now, and writing them down is what stops them being rediscovered as surprises after launch.

Signals worth monitoring

You cannot read messages. What you can watch:

  • Redemption attempts per IP and per code — enumeration looks like this.
  • Reports per token and per holder identity.
  • Tokens revoked within minutes of first redemption — a strong signal that something went wrong immediately.
  • Blocks per holder across different users. A person blocked by several unrelated users is the clearest abuse signal available to a system that cannot read content.
  • Message and call volume that jumps against a token’s own history.

2 · The report pipeline — 🔴 specify it end to end

REPORT                  the user taps Report on a message or a
                        conversation. Available in the app AND on
                        the redemption page -- a holder can be the
                        one being harassed.

PACKAGE                 the device assembles what the server cannot
                        see for itself: the message text, the
                        surrounding messages for context, and proof
                        that the sender really sent it. The server
                        cannot decrypt, so the reporter supplies
                        the evidence.

SERVER RECORD           one row: who reported, which conversation,
                        which token, when, the category chosen, the
                        free text, and the packaged evidence. This
                        is the step the prototype was missing.

TRIAGE QUEUE            it appears somewhere a human will see it,
                        with an SLA clock started.

DECISION                from the matrix in section 4.

ACTION                  applied, and recorded on the report row.

NOTIFY REPORTER         they are told something happened. Not the
                        details of the other person, just that the
                        report was read and acted on.
Confirm the report captures enough to act on

Test it, do not read it. File a report from a test account and then look at what arrived. Ask yourself, with only what is in front of you: could I decide this?

Common gaps, all of which look fine in a screenshot:

  • The reported message is included but the previous three are not, so you cannot tell provocation from harassment.
  • The report identifies the conversation but not which party is being reported.
  • There is no proof of authorship, so a reporter could paste anything and you would have no way to tell.
  • The category is free text, so nothing is countable and every report reads differently.

The authorship proof matters more here than in an ordinary product. Because the server never saw the message, your only account of it comes from someone with a motive. A signature the sender produced is what turns a report from an allegation into evidence.

Triage tooling

A shared inbox is acceptable at launch. A spreadsheet is not. The difference is not sophistication, it is that an inbox has a per-item state and an audit trail — you can see what is unread, what is assigned, and what was said — and a spreadsheet silently loses all three the first time two people edit it.

Minimum requirements: every report gets an id, a state, an owner, and a timestamped history. Reports must be searchable by holder, by token, and by reporter, because the fourth or fifth report about the same person is the one that matters.

3 · The 24-hour SLA, in practice 🔴

Apple requires it, so you have already promised it. Write down what it actually means:

QuestionA workable answer for two founders
What counts as a response?Acknowledged, assessed, and an interim protective action taken if warranted. Not a completed investigation
Who is on duty?A named default and a named backup, written down, swapped explicitly
Weekends and holidays?Covered. The clock does not observe them
What wakes someone?A push alert on a new report, not an inbox someone means to check
Escalation?No response in 4 hours → alert the backup. No response in 12 → alert both, loudly
Both unreachable?An automatic interim action fires. See below
The mechanism that keeps the promise when nobody is awake

For a two-person company, the only honest way to guarantee a 24-hour response is to make part of it automatic. On a report of the most serious categories, suspend the reported party’s access to that conversation pending review, immediately, and tell the reporter that this has happened.

It is reversible, it is proportionate, it is exactly the interim action a human would take anyway, and it converts a staffing promise into a system property.

The cost, stated plainly so it can be reversed if it proves wrong: it is abusable. A malicious reporter can silence someone for a few hours. That is a real harm and it is much smaller than the alternative, which is a serious report sitting unread overnight. Track how often it is used and by whom; a reporter who triggers it repeatedly against different people is themselves a signal.

4 · The triage decision matrix

Write it once so that decisions stay consistent when you are tired, and so that the second founder decides the same way the first one would.

HarassmentBlock the person for that user, across all their tokensBan; retain evidence

Add a worked example under each row — a real anonymised case, or an invented one before you have real ones. A matrix of category names is interpreted differently by two people; a matrix with examples is not. This is the cheapest consistency mechanism you will ever build.

🔴 Blocking must target the person, not the token

This is the requirement most likely to be quietly wrong in the build, because the obvious implementation is wrong in the right-looking way.

A block attached to a token ends when that token does. The blocked person redeems a different token from the same user — or the user issues a new one to someone else and the blocked person gets hold of it — and they are back. From the user’s point of view they blocked someone and the block evaporated.

A block must attach to the holder identity, across every token that user has issued or will issue. Ask for it explicitly, and then prove it:

TEST: a block survives the token it was made on

1. User A issues token T1. Person B redeems it. They exchange
   messages.
2. A blocks B.
   EXPECT: B can no longer send on T1.
3. A issues a new token T2 and gives the code to B.
4. B redeems T2 from the same device and browser.
   EXPECT: refused, or connected-but-silenced by the block.
   FAIL:   B can message A again.
5. Repeat step 4 from a different browser with the same
   identity signal the product uses.
   EXPECT: same result as step 4.
6. A unblocks B.
   EXPECT: B can use T2. T1, being revoked or spent, stays dead.

Step 5 is the one that finds the shallow implementation. Run this before every release that touches blocking, and keep the result — it is part of your UGC evidence for both stores.

5 · Crisis handling

Three protocols, written in advance, each one page. You will read them exactly once, at the worst possible moment, and that is when a page you wrote calmly is worth more than any amount of judgement under stress.

Threats of violence

1. Preserve everything. Do not delete, do not let the account
   be deleted, until this is resolved.
2. Suspend the threatening party's access immediately.
3. Tell the person threatened what you have done, in plain
   language, and that they may want to contact the police.
4. Decide on disclosure -- see "emergency disclosure" below.
5. Record it: what was said, what you did, when, who decided.

Self-harm disclosure

This is not an enforcement matter. Do not suspend anyone.

1. Respond to the person, briefly and without judgement.
2. Provide the helpline numbers -- prepared in advance, in
   English and Hindi, for the region.
3. Do not promise confidentiality you cannot keep, and do not
   attempt to counsel. You are not equipped and it is not
   the role.
4. Record only what is necessary, and keep it tightly held.

Compile the helpline numbers now and check them annually. Looking them up during an incident is exactly the wrong time.

Child safety

1. Preserve. Do not view further than needed to confirm.
2. Suspend immediately, both directions.
3. Escalate the SAME DAY through the reporting path you
   identified in advance. Identify it in advance.
4. Only the named decider handles this. It is not delegated
   and it is not discussed in a group chat.
5. Follow legal advice on retention -- normal deletion rules
   may not apply.

Emergency disclosure

There will be a case where someone appears to be in immediate danger and the ordinary legal request process is too slow. Decide now:

  • Who can make the call. One named person, plus one named deputy. Not “the founders”.
  • On what threshold. A genuine belief of a risk to life, written down at the time.
  • What can be disclosed. Only what you have — which for Token is metadata, not message contents. The lists in lesson 0002 apply here too, and the limits do not relax in an emergency because they are physical rather than procedural.
  • What gets recorded. Everything, contemporaneously, including the reasoning.

Compile authority contact details before they are needed, and keep them somewhere reachable from a phone.

What this costs you

  • A daily duty from launch day. Triage is the one item in this module that never has a quiet week once you have users.
  • Engineering work that is invisible in a demo — the report record, the evidence packaging, the person-level block. It is easy to deprioritise and it is the part the stores audit.
  • Exposure to distressing material, occasionally, with no team to share it with. Take that seriously in advance, including for yourself.
  • An automatic suspension that will sometimes be wrong. Chosen deliberately over the alternative of a serious report going unread overnight. Measure it, and change it if the evidence says the trade was wrong.
